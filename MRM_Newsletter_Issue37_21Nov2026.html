Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

Pillar in focus: Premium (ERP) — Score 10.0/10, WoW unchanged. With zero WoW movement across all pillars this week, we return to the pillar that remains the single largest contributor to the composite: the Equity Risk Premium, which has been pinned at its ceiling score for multiple consecutive weeks.

The ERP reads −0.93%, meaning the implied forward earnings yield on broad US equities now sits 93 basis points below the risk-free rate offered by intermediate Treasuries. In plain terms, the market is paying a premium to hold equity risk rather than demanding compensation for it. This condition has historically been rare and short-lived, occurring briefly in late 1999–2000 and in pockets of 2024–2025 as mega-cap concentration distorted aggregate index valuations.

A negative risk premium does not mechanically cause a selloff. What it does is eliminate the margin of safety. Any exogenous shock — a geopolitical event, a credit accident, a data surprise — arrives into a market that has already priced perfection and has no valuation cushion to absorb it. The Sentinel trigger on this pillar (threshold 0.80%) has been in alert for an extended period, confirming that the reading is not noise but a sustained structural condition.

This is the pillar that makes the Turbulence regime appropriate on the fragility gauge. It is also the pillar that would improve most rapidly in a correction — as equities reprice lower, the implied yield rises and the premium widens — which is precisely why the composite is designed to fall into a crisis rather than rise through one.

CIO Verdict

A high fragility score with Gauge B silent is the system working as designed, not a contradiction. Gauge A at 7.0 says the accumulated structural risk — principally a negative equity risk premium and extreme liquidity readings — is as elevated as the model has measured in its full backtest window. Gauge B says none of that fragility has begun to express itself in real-economy stress: the labor market is tight, delinquencies are falling, and no recessionary process is underway. The portfolio therefore holds its Turbulence-regime weights, which accept moderate equity exposure while maintaining meaningful allocations to sovereigns, credit, and cash.

We note, plainly, that we would be more defensive than the current allocation. A 41.5% equity weight in a negative-ERP environment provides no margin of error, and the gap between Gauge A's warning and Gauge B's silence could close faster than quarterly data can capture. The system's strength is that it does not act on that opinion — it waits for hard evidence in the concurrent triggers. That discipline has kept it out of the 2011, 2018, and 2022 bear markets where premature defensiveness would have been costly. We accept the trade-off and hold.
