Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

Why this pillar. All five pillars moved exactly zero week-over-week, so there is no largest mover in the conventional sense. We focus instead on the pillar that carries the most weight in the current fragility reading: the Equity Risk Premium, which has scored a ceiling 10.0 for multiple consecutive weeks and, alongside Liquidity, is responsible for pulling the composite into the Turbulence band.

What -1.25% means. A negative equity risk premium indicates that the earnings yield on the broad equity market sits below the risk-free rate. In plain terms, investors are paying more per unit of expected cash flow in equities than they would receive by holding sovereign duration of comparable maturity. This is not unprecedented — the late-1990s printed sustained negative readings — but it is historically rare outside of bubble-adjacent conditions and marks a state in which the compensation for bearing equity risk has been fully exhausted.

Mechanical implications. Because the ERP is already at the scoring ceiling, any further compression in the premium cannot raise the pillar score; it can only be resolved by either a rise in earnings or a decline in prices. In a downturn, equity multiples contract and the premium rebuilds mechanically, which would cause this pillar to improve — lowering the composite score — precisely when conditions feel worst. This is the counter-intuitive dynamic that makes Gauge A a fragility measure, not a crisis alarm.

Sentinel confirmation. The ERP sentinel (threshold: 0.80%) has been in alert since the premium crossed zero. It is the only active sentinel of the three monitored. Initial claims at 199K and unemployment at 4.2% remain well below their respective thresholds, confirming that the labour market has not yet transmitted any stress signal.

CIO Verdict

Fragility is fully priced in the score; stress is fully absent from the data. The composite has been at 7.0 for two consecutive weeks with zero movement across all five pillars. This is a market that has built significant vulnerability — a negative equity risk premium, tight liquidity conditions, a barely positive yield curve — without yet generating a single concurrent-stress trigger. The portfolio holds its macro weights because the rules say to hold them, and this week there was nothing in the data to challenge that instruction.

The ERP sentinel remains the lone alert. Until the labour market deteriorates or bank credit quality turns, Gauge B will stay silent and the allocation will not shift. The risk, stated plainly, is that the next move in the composite is down — not because conditions improve, but because they worsen and the pillars mechanically reverse. When that happens, the score will fall and the portfolio will already be positioned for it through the Gauge B override. Until then: hold, observe, and resist the urge to front-run a stress event the data has not yet confirmed.
