Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

No pillar moved this week. In the absence of a WoW differential to anchor the deep dive, we focus on the pillar carrying the most extreme score: the equity risk premium, which has been pinned at 10.0/10 — the maximum fragility reading — for multiple consecutive weeks.

At –1.42%, the implied equity risk premium is negative. This means the earnings yield on broad US equities sits below the risk-free rate offered by intermediate-duration government bonds. In the 2005–2026 backtest, a negative ERP has occurred only during late-cycle euphoria or when monetary policy rates lagged inflation expectations. Neither condition has historically resolved without a repricing of either the equity numerator (earnings) or the denominator (multiples).

The 10.0/10 score is a ceiling, not a prediction. It states that the margin of safety embedded in equity prices is, by this measure, nonexistent. It does not state when — or whether — a correction occurs. The Solvency pillar, by contrast, sits at 2.5/10, reflecting corporate balance sheets that remain sound. These two pillars pulling in opposite directions is characteristic of late-cycle conditions: strong fundamentals funding expensive valuations.

The portfolio's response to this tension is structural, not discretionary. The Turbulence regime holds equities at 41.5% — a meaningful underweight relative to a pure market-cap benchmark — while maintaining a 14.5% cash buffer and 19.7% in duration. If and when Gauge B fires, the allocation shifts to the Critical vector. Until then, the current weights stand.

CIO Verdict

Unchanged data. Unchanged allocation. Unchanged risk.

The system this week is a study in stasis. Every pillar, every sentinel, every trigger reads the same as seven days ago. The Resilience Score at 7.0 continues to register the highest sustained fragility load in the backtest history — driven almost entirely by the liquidity and premium pillars, both of which sit at or near their ceilings. Yet Gauge B, the only gauge that governs the portfolio regime, remains silent. This is the system working as designed: Gauge A says there is a great deal to go wrong; Gauge B says none of it is going wrong yet.

The negative equity risk premium is the single most uncomfortable data point in the dashboard. Equities are priced to underperform the risk-free rate. In an environment where cash yields exceed the implied equity return, the 41.5% equity allocation is, by this measure, a drag on expected returns relative to a pure cash position. The system holds the equity weight because Gauge B is off and the Turbulence vector is the operative allocation — not because the valuation picture is encouraging. Were this a discretionary mandate, a further trim toward cash would be defensible. It is not a discretionary mandate.

The portfolio trails its equity benchmark by 10.77 percentage points since inception. That gap is the cost of diversification in a market that has rewarded concentration. The 5.49% absolute return over the period is positive; the relative underperformance reflects an equity market that has extended its premium compression beyond anything the backtest anticipated. The system does not chase. It waits for Gauge B. When — not if — a stress event fires, the reallocation will be mechanical, immediate, and indifferent to where the Resilience Score happens to be at that moment.

Posture: hold all positions. Monitor Gauge B weekly. No discretionary action.
