Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

Why this pillar. With every pillar registering zero week-on-week change, no single factor is the "biggest WoW mover" in a strict sense. We therefore focus on the pillar that contributes the most to composite fragility: the Equity Risk Premium, which sits at the maximum score of 10.0/10 and has been there for multiple weeks.

What it says. The ERP reading is −1.20%. A negative equity risk premium means the earnings yield on the broad equity market is below the risk-free rate — investors are accepting a negative excess return for bearing equity risk. This has historically been a reliable marker of valuation extremes, observed in the late 1990s and briefly in early 2020 before rapid monetary easing compressed real yields.

Why it matters. A negative ERP does not mean equities decline tomorrow. It means the margin of safety embedded in price is zero. Any shock — earnings disappointment, a liquidity event, a geopolitical rupture — meets a market that has no valuation cushion to absorb it. The pillar contributes 10.0 out of a possible 10 to the composite, and together with Liquidity at 9.5, accounts for the bulk of the elevated fragility reading.

What it does not mean. This pillar is a leading indicator of vulnerability, not a timing signal. Backtested, a 10.0 ERP score has persisted for quarters before a correction materialised. It is a structural condition, not a catalyst. Gauge B exists precisely to identify when accumulated fragility begins to discharge into real-economy damage. Until Gauge B fires, the portfolio acknowledges the risk through its Turbulence allocation but does not adopt a defensive posture.

Sentinel status. The ERP Sentinel is the sole active alert among the three monitored thresholds. It triggered at −1.20% against a 0.80% threshold — the premium would need to widen by over 200 basis points to clear the alert. Initial Jobless Claims (200K vs. 275K threshold) and Unemployment Rate (4.1% vs. 5.2% threshold) remain well inside safe bounds.

CIO Verdict

Static fragility, zero concurrent stress, no action. The system is in a familiar configuration: Gauge A reads 7.0 — significant accumulated vulnerability driven by extreme valuation compression and tight liquidity conditions — while Gauge B reads OFF, with both triggers dormant. This is not a contradiction; it is the design. Fragility is a state. A crisis is an event. The portfolio does not pre-empt the event; it prices the state.

The ERP Sentinel remains the sole active alert. At −1.20%, the equity market is offering investors a negative premium for bearing risk. This is a structural observation, not a tactical signal. The labour market — claims at 200K, unemployment at 4.1% — shows no deterioration whatsoever. Until these real-economy channels begin to transmit stress, the Turbulence allocation holds.

The portfolio trails the broad equity market by 9.1 percentage points, which is the direct cost of holding a diversified, risk-managed allocation during a period of concentrated equity strength driven by precisely the valuation dynamics that Gauge A flags as fragile. The alpha deficit is a feature of the posture, not a failure of the system. When fragility eventually resolves — whether through a benign repricing or a disorderly correction — the allocation's defensive tilt will either narrow the gap or reverse it entirely. Until then, we hold.
