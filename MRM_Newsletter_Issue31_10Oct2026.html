Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

Why this pillar. No pillar moved week-over-week — all five registered 0.0 changes. In a flat-delta environment the appropriate focus is the pillar that carries the most extreme reading and therefore contributes the most marginal risk to the composite. That pillar is the equity risk premium, pinned at a perfect 10.0/10.

What it says. The ERP stands at –1.44%. A negative risk premium means that, on the model's inputs, the expected real return on equities over the intermediate horizon falls below the risk-free rate. Investors are, in aggregate, paying for the privilege of holding equity risk. This is not unprecedented — the late-1990s episode and pockets of 2021 produced similar readings — but it represents the kind of valuation asymmetry that historically resolves through either a sharp multiple compression or an extended period of below-trend returns.

What it does not say. A negative ERP is not a timing signal. The 2024 Sentinel alert on this metric fired while equities continued to advance for months. The pillar measures stored energy, not the spark. That is precisely why Gauge B exists as a separate mechanism: until concurrent stress materialises in the labour market or the banking system, the portfolio does not de-risk. The ERP alert within the Sentinel framework is active (True), but it is only one of three sentinels and carries no standalone portfolio authority.

Institutional implication. The premium compression means that the margin of safety in risk assets is thin. Any exogenous shock — a liquidity event, a geopolitical dislocation, or an upside surprise in unemployment claims — would be absorbed by a market with essentially no valuation cushion. The Turbulence allocation already reflects this via a reduced equity weight relative to an all-weather posture, but the 41.5% equity share still carries meaningful drawdown exposure should conditions deteriorate.

CIO Verdict

The system is in a state of elevated latent fragility with zero concurrent stress. Gauge A at 7.0 flags a market environment where valuations offer no margin of safety and liquidity conditions are stretched — the kind of backdrop where shocks propagate faster and further than participants expect. Gauge B at OFF confirms that no shock is propagating now. These are not contradictory readings; they are two instruments measuring different things.

The portfolio holds. No rebalance was triggered, no transactions were executed. The 41.5% equity weight is, in my assessment, generous given a negative risk premium, but it is the weight the Turbulence regime prescribes and the system is rules-based for a reason: discretionary overrides in calm periods erode the discipline that protects capital in crisis periods. The –11.31% alpha shortfall versus the broad equity benchmark is the cost of that discipline in a momentum-driven market. It is a cost I accept.

What would change the posture: a Sahm rule reading approaching 0.30 would warrant heightened monitoring; a bank delinquency print above +0.40 pp would signal credit deterioration well before the formal trigger. Neither is in evidence. Until Gauge B fires, the allocation stands.
