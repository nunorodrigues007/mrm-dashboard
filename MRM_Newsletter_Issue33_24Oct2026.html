Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

Why this pillar: With all five pillars unchanged week-over-week, there is no single "biggest WoW mover." We focus instead on the pillar that contributes the most to the composite and anchors the Turbulence regime: the equity risk premium, scored at its theoretical ceiling of 10.0/10.

The reading: The forward earnings yield on US large-cap equities sits 1.16 percentage points below the 10-year Treasury yield. This is a negative equity risk premium — investors are paying more per unit of expected earnings in equities than they would earn risk-free in government bonds. The premium has been negative for multiple consecutive months, a condition last seen in the late 1990s.

What it means for fragility: A negative ERP does not predict timing. It measures the cushion available to absorb disappointment. When earnings estimates are revised down, or when rates move up, a negative ERP means the equity market has no valuation buffer. The correction, when it comes, must be larger to re-establish a positive premium. This is the definition of stored fragility.

What it does not mean: It is not a sell signal. Negative ERP regimes have historically persisted for quarters, even years, while equities continued to grind higher on momentum and earnings growth. The pillar measures vulnerability, not direction. The portfolio acknowledges the risk through reduced equity exposure relative to a full-risk allocation, but does not abandon the asset class.

Sentinel status: The ERP sentinel — which fires when the premium drops below 0.80% — is the only active alert in the system. It has been active for an extended period and is now deeply through its threshold at -1.16%. This sentinel informs the composite score; it does not, by itself, trigger a regime change. That function belongs exclusively to Gauge B.

CIO Verdict

Unchanged data, unchanged posture. The complete absence of week-over-week movement across all five pillars, all three sentinels, and both Gauge B triggers produces the clearest possible signal: do nothing. The system is in a holding pattern, and the appropriate response is to mirror it.

The latent risk is real. A negative equity risk premium, net liquidity at 335% of GDP, and a debt-service ratio at 11.1% are not benign readings. They describe a system where a modest shock — an earnings disappointment, a liquidity event, a sovereign-funding dislocation — would find little buffer. But "little buffer" is not the same as "imminent event." The labour market is tight. Bank balance sheets are clean. Consumption continues.

The portfolio carries 41.5% in equities, 14.5% in cash, and 19.7% in duration — a posture that accepts the ride while keeping dry powder. If Gauge B fires, the shift to Critical is mechanical and immediate. Until then, patience is the position. The next scheduled semi-annual rebalance is 29 January 2027. We wait.
