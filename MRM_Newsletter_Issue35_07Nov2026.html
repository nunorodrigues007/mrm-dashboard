Executive Summary

The Global Resilience Score holds at 7.0/10 for a second consecutive week, deep inside the Turbulence band with all five pillars active and not one of them moving. The composite is flat because nothing in the underlying data changed: the labour market is neither loosening nor tightening, the curve remains barely positive, and the valuation and liquidity pillars continue to register at extremes. A frozen dashboard at an elevated level is not reassuring — it means the conditions that pushed fragility readings higher are persisting rather than resolving.

Two pillars — Equity Risk Premium and Liquidity — remain in critical territory, scoring 9.5 and 10.0 respectively. The negative risk premium (-0.83%) is the single largest contributor to the composite; equities are priced to deliver less than Treasuries on a forward basis, a condition that has historically preceded either a sharp correction in multiples or a sustained period of below-trend returns. Liquidity stress at 335.5% of the threshold confirms that the monetary plumbing is tight enough to amplify any shock, even if no shock is present today.

Crucially, the portfolio regime is not set by this score. Gauge B — Concurrent Stress — remains OFF. Neither the Sahm rule nor bank delinquency data has breached its trigger. The system therefore holds the Turbulence allocation without modification. A 7.0 fragility reading with no concurrent stress firing is the system working as designed: it is flagging that the margin for error is narrow, while acknowledging that the economy has not yet cracked.

Deep Dive — Equity Risk Premium

No pillar moved this week. All five WoW deltas are exactly zero. In the absence of a clear mover, the deep dive defaults to the pillar carrying the highest absolute score: the Equity Risk Premium, which pins at 10.0/10 — the ceiling of the fragility scale.

The raw reading is -0.83%, meaning the earnings yield on broad US equities now sits 83 basis points below the risk-free rate available on intermediate Treasuries. This is not a marginal overshoot. The ERP threshold embedded in the sentinel framework is +0.80%; the current print is 163 basis points below that line. Investors are accepting a negative premium for bearing equity duration — a posture historically associated with late-cycle euphoria or structural re-rating episodes that eventually reverse.

Mechanically, the negative ERP is the product of two forces: equity multiples that have continued to expand through 2026, and a nominal Treasury yield curve that, while barely positive, still offers a meaningful coupon. The combination means the forward return expectation for equities, as implied by current prices, is lower than what an investor can lock in risk-free. This does not predict the timing of a correction — the ERP was negative for stretches in the late 1990s before the market topped — but it does mean the margin of safety in equity allocations is negligible.

For the portfolio, this pillar is the primary reason the composite sits at 7.0 rather than in the 4–5 range where Cycle and Solvency alone would place it. It is also why the Turbulence allocation already underweights equities relative to a pure growth portfolio. Should the ERP revert toward its sentinel threshold — via either a selloff in equities or a decline in Treasury yields — the composite would fall materially, which would be a sign of improving resilience, not deterioration.

CIO Verdict

Fragility persists; the economy does not yet confirm it. The system is in the state it was designed for: a high leading score — driven almost entirely by valuation excess and liquidity tightness — paired with a concurrent-stress gauge that refuses to fire. This is uncomfortable by construction. The composite is telling us the runway is short; the triggers are telling us the plane has not stalled.

The unchanged readings across all five pillars and all three sentinels this week are themselves informative. When nothing moves, it usually means the forces pushing toward fragility and the forces restraining it are in temporary equilibrium. Claims at 189K and unemployment at 4.1% keep the labour market firmly in expansion territory. The negative ERP and critical liquidity score keep the fragility composite elevated. Until one side breaks — either claims spike toward 275K and the Sahm rule approaches 0.50, or multiples compress and the ERP reverts positive — the allocation holds.

No transactions were executed this week. The next scheduled semi-annual rebalance falls on 29 January 2027. Between now and then, only a Gauge B firing would force a reallocation. The portfolio carries a +4.55% cumulative return against -6.52% alpha versus the broad equity benchmark — a predictable consequence of holding 41.5% equities rather than 100% during a period of persistent index appreciation driven by the same multiple expansion that makes the ERP negative. The underperformance is the cost of the hedge. Whether the hedge pays depends on what happens to that runway.
